The most plausible paper here is not “method X wins,” because SlimQwen already makes that hard. The strongest angle is: under a fixed expert-only 0.75 retain ratio on a small no-shared-expert MoE, what matters for the first `10^2–10^4` recovery steps, what reorders during training, and what property at step 0 predicts that trajectory.

1. **Recovery-Trajectory Atlas.** Summary: Build dense recovery curves for representative compression families and treat `AURC` plus crossover time as the primary outcome, not just step-0 or 10k-step accuracy. Hypothesis: the training-free ranking will not be stable; some methods will start better but be overtaken within `500–2k` steps because they preserve accuracy but not trainability, while gentler distortions preserve an easier optimization basin. Minimum viable experiment: pick `6–8` representatives at the same `0.75` retain ratio: SlimQwen prune/merge variants, per-expert SVD/Nystrom, per-expert SparseGPT, and one random or magnitude control; evaluate at `0/100/250/500/1k/2k/5k/10k` steps on MMLU/GSM8K. Contribution: empirical-finding. Risk: LOW, because even “no crossovers” is a clean negative result. Effort: `1–2` weeks. Strongest objection: “this is just a benchmark.” SlimQwen threat: real, but limited; they study endpoint closure within whole-expert methods at very large scale, not short-horizon crossovers across compression families in a no-shared-expert MoE.

2. **What Predicts Recoverability?** Summary: Test which step-0 property predicts `500-step`, `2k-step`, and `10k-step` value: raw reconstruction, calibration loss, MoE-block output KL, routed-token Fisher-weighted error, gradient alignment, diversity retention, effective rank. Hypothesis: step-0 task score and plain local reconstruction will predict immediate damage but not short recovery; routed-token curvature/alignment metrics will predict trajectory better because they capture what SGD can cheaply repair. Minimum viable experiment: collect `20–30` compressed checkpoints from multiple methods and hyperparameters, compute the above diagnostics before training, then regress them against early slope and AURC. Contribution: diagnostic. Risk: MEDIUM, because it can degenerate into correlation fishing. Effort: `1–2` weeks once checkpoints exist. Strongest objection: “too many metrics, weak causal story.” SlimQwen threat: this survives it; if endpoints tie, the natural next question is which step-0 property predicts who gets there fastest.

3. **Why Does the Gap Close: Rerouting vs Relearning?** Summary: Decompose recovery into router reallocation, surviving-expert takeover, and damaged-expert relearning. Hypothesis: early recovery is mostly rerouting toward less-damaged surviving experts, while later recovery is true expert relearning; the split should be sharper here because there is no shared expert safety net. Minimum viable experiment: choose `3` contrasting compressed models and run short recovery under `full update`, `router frozen`, and `experts frozen except router` (or as close as the trainer allows); track routing entropy, expert utilization shifts, and per-expert gradient norms for `500–2k` steps. Contribution: diagnostic/mechanistic. Risk: MEDIUM, because reviewers may dislike the auxiliary training settings. Effort: `1–2` weeks. Strongest objection: “this changes the recovery protocol.” SlimQwen threat: not defeated; it directly tests whether the router-repair story from prior work explains closure when the router was never compressed and the model has no shared expert.

4. **Family Matters More Than Criterion.** Summary: Ask whether compression family explains more variance than the exact scoring rule within a family. Hypothesis: within-family differences collapse quickly after recovery, but family-level structure remains because whole-expert deletion/merging loses specialization differently from low-rank or unstructured distortion. Minimum viable experiment: compare `2–3` methods in each family under matched calibration budget and exact retain ratio, then run variance decomposition on step-0, `2k`, and `10k` results. Contribution: empirical-finding. Risk: LOW-MEDIUM. Effort: `~2` weeks. Strongest objection: “taxonomy is not novelty.” SlimQwen threat: this is precisely the missing axis in SlimQwen, which does not cover intra-expert low-rank or unstructured compression.

5. **Layer Allocation Dominates Method Choice.** Summary: At fixed global `0.75` retain ratio, layerwise budget allocation may matter more than the compression rule itself. Hypothesis: short SFT can repair local weight distortion but not missing capacity in the wrong layers; mid/late MoE layers are likely the bottleneck for GSM8K recovery. Minimum viable experiment: compare one naive method and one stronger method under `uniform` vs `sensitivity-weighted` per-layer budgets derived from calibration loss or Fisher. If naive+good allocation beats smart+uniform, that is a sharp result. Contribution: empirical-finding / light new-method. Risk: MEDIUM. Effort: `1–2` weeks. Strongest objection: “you changed the problem from method design to budget allocation.” SlimQwen threat: weak; they co-vary many architectural knobs and do not isolate expert-only layer allocation.

6. **Diversity Retention Predicts Reasoning Recovery.** Summary: Test whether preserving inter-expert diversity matters more for GSM8K than for MMLU. Hypothesis: local reconstruction can restore average factual behavior, but collapsed expert specialization is hard to recreate from only `10k` SFT samples, so GSM8K should remain sensitive to diversity loss after MMLU has mostly recovered. Minimum viable experiment: compare merge-heavy methods against low-rank/unstructured ones, and correlate GSM8K recovery with pairwise expert similarity, token-specialization entropy, and expert-cluster spectrum. Contribution: diagnostic / empirical-finding. Risk: MEDIUM. Effort: `1–2` weeks. Strongest objection: “diversity is a post hoc story.” SlimQwen threat: limited; their endpoint claim does not rule out task-specific trajectory differences driven by specialization loss.

7. **One-Step Recoverability as a Selection Rule.** Summary: Pick compressed checkpoints by “loss after `1–5` SGD steps on `~512` OpenThoughts samples” instead of by training-free score. Hypothesis: the short-recovery winner is the checkpoint in the right basin, not the checkpoint with the best immediate zero-shot metrics. Minimum viable experiment: generate `10–20` variants within one family, rank them by step-0 score, by reconstruction error, and by one-step loss; test which ranking best predicts `500-step` or `2k-step` performance. Contribution: diagnostic / light new-method. Risk: MEDIUM. Effort: `~1` week. Strongest objection: “you leaked the recovery distribution into model selection.” SlimQwen threat: it does not address this regime; their claim is about eventual closure, not whether a tiny amount of training reveals the true winner.

8. **Fisher-Weighted Local Reconstruction.** Summary: Build a minimal new within-expert compressor that preserves high-curvature routed directions rather than plain activation error. Hypothesis: dense-model evidence says plain local reconstruction washes out criterion differences after retraining; in MoE, curvature-weighting should matter precisely when the recovery budget is short and routing makes some directions much harder to relearn. Minimum viable experiment: implement Fisher-weighted rank selection or Fisher-weighted low-rank reconstruction on a subset of layers first, then compare against unweighted SVD/Nystrom at the same retain ratio for `0/500/2k` steps. Contribution: new-method. Risk: MEDIUM-HIGH, because the effect could be small and implementation is nontrivial. Effort: `2–3` weeks. Strongest objection: “incremental weighting trick.” SlimQwen threat: low; they do not probe curvature-aware intra-expert reconstruction at all.

9. **Heterogeneity-Aware Hybrid Compression.** Summary: Use different compression families for different experts instead of one policy everywhere. Hypothesis: high-frequency/high-rank experts should be compressed smoothly with low-rank methods, while low-frequency but idiosyncratic experts should be partially preserved rather than merged away; one-size-fits-all loses either trainability or specialization. Minimum viable experiment: bucket experts by routing frequency and effective rank, assign a simple family per bucket under the same global budget, and compare against the best pure-family baseline. Contribution: new-method / empirical-finding. Risk: MEDIUM. Effort: `~2` weeks. Strongest objection: “heuristic mixture of tricks.” SlimQwen threat: their “no single method wins” actually motivates a heterogeneity-aware hybrid instead of defeating it.

10. **Partial Preservation Is the Hidden Variable.** Summary: Test whether preserving a small dense spine inside every expert, not the prune/merge criterion, is what makes naive methods recover well. Hypothesis: once every expert retains a minimally trainable core, short recovery becomes easy and criterion-level differences mostly disappear; without that core, gaps widen sharply. Minimum viable experiment: cross partial-preservation fraction `{0, small, medium}` with `2–3` compression families at fixed `0.75` retain ratio and compare step-0 plus `500/2k` recovery. Contribution: empirical-finding / diagnostic. Risk: LOW-MEDIUM. Effort: `~1` week. Strongest objection: “baseline-specific ablation.” SlimQwen threat: this is exactly why it is valuable; it converts their broad endpoint claim into a mechanistic statement you can directly test in a no-shared-expert model.

**Top 3 I’d actually run**
1. **Recovery-Trajectory Atlas.** This is the foundation. It directly targets the only novelty axis SlimQwen leaves open: short-horizon reordering across method families. It is also low risk; even a flat negative result is publishable if the study is clean.
2. **What Predicts Recoverability?** This is the highest-upside diagnostic. If one step-0 property predicts AURC or `2k-step` value, that is more important than another compressor. If nothing predicts it well, that negative result is also useful and strengthens the case that step-0 evaluation is the wrong selection target.
3. **Partial Preservation Is the Hidden Variable** or **Layer Allocation Dominates Method Choice.** If you want the cheapest sharp result, run the partial-preservation ablation. If you want the broader paper, run layer-allocation instead. Both directly answer “what really matters” and are not invalidated by “the gap eventually closes.”

If I had to bet on the outcome: endpoint gaps at `10k` samples will mostly compress, but early curves will not be rank-stable; family-level structure and partial-preservation/allocation choices will matter more than fine-grained importance criteria, and the best predictor will be some routed-token curvature/alignment signal rather than raw reconstruction error.
