es_token learning rate: what works, and how to measure it -- 2026-08-23
=======================================================================
Qwen3-4B teacher -> Qwen3-1.7B student, MATH lv3-5, batch 64 x 1024, N=8 rails,
pack_width=64, sigma=0.01 absolute, student_iw, token_agg=mean, TEMPERATURE=1.0.

THE MEASUREMENT TRAP (cost me a wrongly-killed run)
---------------------------------------------------
train/L_clean_mean is computed on whatever 64 prompts THAT STEP drew. On this
dataset it swings 0.23 - 3.4 from batch to batch, which is far larger than any
learning-rate effect. Three LRs spanning 100x produce indistinguishable curves:

  step   LR=1e-3   LR=1e-5   LR=3e-5
    0    0.22714   0.22714   0.22714     <- identical (pre-update)
    1    3.19522   2.84964   2.84373
    3    2.86463   3.37455   3.34720
    5    0.27865   0.22712   0.22526     <- low in ALL runs = same easy batch
    7    2.65108   3.22027   3.09948
The applied update differs 100x (lr*dW ~= 2.2 vs 0.02) yet the curve is the same
shape. The swings are DATA, not the optimizer. dW_norm_mean is likewise no
divergence signal: it is the gradient-estimate norm BEFORE the LR multiplies it,
so it is similar across LRs by construction.

USE eval/heldout_clean_loss -- a FIXED 16-prompt probe (ray_trainer.py:333).
It is logged only inside the eval_interval branch, i.e. every EVAL_INTERVAL
steps, not per step.

PROBE NOISE FLOOR
-----------------
The probe scores SAMPLED rollouts at T=1.0, so it is stochastic even for a frozen
model. Three sweep runs read the SAME untouched step-0 model as
  0.1908 / 0.2126 / 0.2242   -> spread 0.033, about +-8%.
Any within-run move smaller than that is not interpretable.

LR = 1e-3 (the shipped all-layer default) DEGRADES THE MODEL
-------------------------------------------------------------
150-step run, evals every 25 steps -- monotonic on BOTH fixed-set metrics:
  step                0        25        50
  probe KL         0.2244    0.5565    1.1559     (2.5x then 2.1x per interval)
  MATH-500 (200)     5.0%      1.5%      0.0%
Far outside the +-8% noise floor. Killed at step 50.
Why 1e-3 was wrong: it was calibrated on the GREEDY benchmark, where
dW_norm_mean ~= 240. At TEMPERATURE=1.0 the rails ride a higher-entropy
trajectory, the importance weights spread, and dW_norm_mean is ~866 at step 0 and
~2000-2550 in steady state -- roughly 3.6x larger before any LR is applied.

SWEEP (21 steps, EVAL_INTERVAL=10, fixed probe + MATH-500 on 50)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
  LR      probe s0    s10      s20     MATH-500 (50 samples)
  1e-4     0.2126   0.1886   0.2035     0.08  0.08  0.10
  1e-5     0.1908   0.2126   0.2010     0.08  0.06  0.08
  1e-6     0.2242   0.1988   0.1909     0.08  0.04  0.10
All three are flat within the +-8% noise floor, and MATH-500 on 50 samples has
sigma ~= 4pp so those columns carry no signal either.

CONCLUSION -- this is a BOUND, not a ranking:
  1e-3 destroys the model; 1e-4 and below do not; 20 steps cannot separate
  1e-4 / 1e-5 / 1e-6.
Launched the 150-step run at 1e-4 = the largest non-degrading LR (most movement
per step while an order of magnitude below what failed). That is a principled
default, NOT a measured optimum. Separating 1e-4 from 1e-5 needs a horizon long
enough for the signal to exceed +-8%, i.e. hundreds of steps, or a lower-variance
probe (greedy probe rollouts, or many more probe prompts).

OPEN
----
- Whether es_token learns at all at any LR is still unanswered; the 150-step
  1e-4 run is the first test with a horizon that could show it.
- BP-OPD baseline (LR 1e-6, 138 steps) was also flat: MATH-500 2.8/2.2/2.8/1.8%
  across its four evals. It is a wall-clock reference, not a learning baseline.
- Both runs cap responses at 1024 tokens and EVERY rollout hits the cap without
  emitting EOS (response_length mean=min=max=1024), so MATH-500 reads near its
  floor for both. Fixing that (longer budget, or a template that terminates) is
  a prerequisite for accuracy being a usable metric here.


150-STEP RUN AT LR=1e-4 -- NEGATIVE RESULT (2026-08-23)
========================================================
wandb zo-opd-q34b-1p7b / N8_pw64_lr1e-4. 150 steps, 37.18 s/step, 92.9 min,
9,713,323 token-records, weight_sync_ok=1.0 throughout.

  step        0      25      50      75     100     125     149
  probe    0.2126  0.2002  0.1987  0.2169  0.2049  0.2157  0.2228
  MATH500   6.0%    7.0%    6.5%    5.5%    7.0%    2.0%    4.0%

es_token does NOT learn measurably at 1e-4 over 150 steps. All seven probe
readings lie in 0.199-0.223 with no direction; the endpoint is +4.8% vs step 0,
which is INSIDE the +-8% noise floor, so the honest statement is "no change",
not "slightly worse". MATH-500 wanders 2-7% with no trend (sigma ~= 1.7pp at
n=200). An apparent monotone decline at steps 25/50 broke at step 75 -- exactly
the false signal the noise floor predicts, and the reason 3-point trends on this
probe must not be reported as progress.

BRACKET ESTABLISHED (still no working recipe):
  LR 1e-3  destroys the model (probe +415% by step 50, accuracy 0%)
  LR 1e-4  holds it steady    (no degradation, no improvement)
One order of magnitude between "destroys" and "does nothing", with no observed
window in which it learns.

NOT ES-SPECIFIC: the BP-OPD baseline was equally flat (MATH-500 2.8/2.2/2.8/1.8%
over 138 steps at LR 1e-6). NEITHER method moved in this setup, which points at
the setup rather than the algorithm.

LEADING SUSPECT -- TRUNCATION. In BOTH runs every rollout hits the 1024-token cap
without emitting EOS (response_length mean=min=max=1024, clip_ratio=1.0), so the
student never produces a terminated answer and the teacher reward is computed
entirely on truncated continuations. MATH-500 therefore reads a floor for both.

PREREQUISITES BEFORE JUDGING es_token AS A METHOD
--------------------------------------------------
1. Fix truncation (longer budget, or a template that terminates). Until then
   neither the KL nor the accuracy metric measures what it should.
2. A probe that can resolve the effect: 16 prompts sampled at T=1.0 gives +-8%.
   Greedy probe rollouts over ~100 prompts would cut that several-fold and cost
   far less than a 93-minute training run.
3. Only then revisit the LR between 1e-4 and 1e-3, where a working step size may
   sit -- the current bracket is too wide to conclude one does not exist.
